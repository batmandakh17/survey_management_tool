Introduction

1What the tool does

The RTM interview tool is one web page, rtm_interview_tool.html, that runs in a web browser on the enumerator's or the leader's own computer. It has two views, chosen with the buttons at the top of the page:

  • Enumerator. Each enumerator opens the assignment file their leader gave them, works through the calls that are due, logs every dial with its time and result, saves their session, and exports their log.
  • Team leader. The leader prepares each round: they assign households to enumerators, plan each household's first dial, and write one assignment file per enumerator. During and after the round, they import the enumerators' exports, settle any merge question, and read progress, the merged call log, the round's analytics and the risk lists.
Offline by design. The page has no server, no shared database and no network connection. The browser is told to refuse every request from it. It reads only the files you open and writes only the files you save or export. Nothing leaves your computer unless you move a file yourself.

The tool follows the handbook's practice. The default way of assigning households keeps the same enumerator, rotates a planned share of households each round, and switches the enumerator for a household that becomes hard to reach (Section 3.5.3). Each household's first dial is planned at the hour of its last interview (Section 3.5.2), early in the round's window (Section 3.4). The definitions of a dial, a complete and an enumerator-day are those of Box 3.0.

2Files and folders

Because nothing is shared over a network, the work moves in files. Three kinds of folder keep them apart:

FolderWho can read itWhat goes in it
The enumerator's own folderThat enumerator and the leaderThe enumerator's assignment file, and their saved sessions
The common folderThe whole teamThe enumerators' exported logs (.csv and .json). They hold household IDs, times and results, but no names or phone numbers
The leader's own folderThe leaderThe household list, the leader's session, the round's manifest and outputs, contacts files
Contact information never goes in the common folder. An assignment file holds names and phone numbers, but only that enumerator's own households. A contacts file, written when an enumerator adds or corrects a number, goes to the leader directly. The leader's session file holds the whole household list.

3A round at a glance

  1. The leader prepares the round (Part B, steps 1 to 8): household list, earlier rounds, replacements, calendar, team, rule. The tool writes one assignment file per enumerator and a manifest of the round.
  2. The leader gives each enumerator their own file, through a folder only that enumerator can read, with its passphrase if the file is protected.
  3. Enumerators call and log (Part A). They save their session whenever they stop, and export their log at the end of each day.
  4. The leader imports the exports whenever they want to see progress, and settles the merge questions.
  5. At the end of the round, the leader imports the final exports and writes the round's files: the merged call log, interviews.csv and calendar.csv for the handbook's round template, and the merge report.

4Before you start

  • Browser. Open the page in Google Chrome, for example by dragging the file onto a Chrome window. Chrome was used for all the tool's tests. Edge, Firefox and Safari should work but have not been tested. In Chrome a save can overwrite the same file; in Firefox and Safari each save downloads a new file with the time in its name.
  • Practice files. The folder samples/ holds the synthetic program L2MN-DEMO used in this manual: assignment files for round 5 in samples/round05/, and the leader's inputs and the enumerators' exports in samples/leader/. Phone numbers there use the invalid country code +000.
  • Clock. The tool records times from the computer's clock. Keep it set to the right date, time and time zone.

Part A · EnumeratorOpening an assignment, calling, logging, saving and exporting

A1Open your assignment

Your team leader gives you an assignment file, named like E07_assignment_R05.json (your enumerator ID and the round). It lists your households for the round, with their phone numbers, their history and the plan for each one's first dial.

  1. Open rtm_interview_tool.html. The Enumerator view opens.
  2. Click Open my assignment file and choose your file.
  3. If the file is protected, the tool asks for its passphrase. Type the passphrase your leader gave you and click Open. The passphrase is used only on your computer and is not stored.

To carry on where you stopped, use Continue a saved session instead (A18).

A2The screen at a glance

The summary at the top gives your enumerator ID, the program and round, the round's window and the working days left. The bar shows the share of your households completed. Under it are the main buttons:

ButtonWhat it doesSee
Save sessionSaves everything you logged into one file in your own folder, so you can close the browser and continue laterA18
Export logWrites your call log as a CSV and a JSON file for the common folderA19
Load earlier logsAdds your exports from earlier rounds, to compare your performanceA16
Load updated assignmentOpens a new version of your assignment for the same round, keeping what you loggedA20
Close sessionCloses the round on this screen; with unsaved changes it offers to save firstA18

The line under the buttons says when you last saved and exported. The tabs below lead to Today, Households, Calendar, My performance and Log.

A3The Today tab

The Today tab lists the calls to make, in four columns:

  • Due now: calls whose time has come, including overdue ones from earlier days.
  • Later today: calls planned for later in the day, in time order.
  • Ahead of plan: when fewer households are due or planned today than your leader's day list holds (the number is given under the columns), households planned for later days, soonest first, so that unanswered calls do not leave you waiting. Work through them once the calls due are done; a booked callback is never pulled forward.
  • Needs your decision: households where the next step is not a call, such as a hard refusal to release or a household with no working number.

A label on each card says what kind of call it is: First dial, Redial, Callback (an agreed time) or Overdue. Use the arrows beside the date to look at another day. Click a card to open the household.

A4A household's panel

Clicking a household opens its panel on the right. From the top:

  • State and reason: not started, in progress, complete, and so on, and why the household is with you (R2). Why is this household with me? explains the rule your leader used.
  • Next: the tool's suggestion for the next call, with its time. It says “overdue since” when the time has passed.
  • Usual time: the hour of the household's last interview, or the hour your leader planned.
  • Last interview and last three rounds: each round's number with a filled dot if the household was interviewed and an empty dot if not; a dash means the household had not joined the panel yet.
  • In the panel since, first dial planned and the sampling unit (PSU and stratum).

A5Make a call

  1. In Phone numbers, find the number to call. Copy puts it on the clipboard. The line under each number says how its last dial went.
  2. Click Start call by that number when you dial. A timer starts.
  3. When the call ends, click End call. If no call was made after all, click Cancel (no call made): nothing is logged.
The start and end times come from the clock, so the call's length is recorded for you. Box 3.0 uses these times: a complete needs at least 3 minutes, and dial minutes count toward productivity.

A6Log the result

After End call, the result form opens.

  1. Choose the result of the call (R1 lists them all). The coloured edge marks the kind: green for a complete, brown for a number problem, red for a refusal.
  2. If the respondent agreed a time to call back, enter it in Callback time. It is required for “Appointment booked”.
  3. In After this call, keep the default Keep calling, or choose Release for this round or Ask for another enumerator next round.
  4. Add a short note if useful. Notes are operational only: never write personal details.
  5. Click Save call. Discard drops the call without logging it.

A7Booked callbacks

A call saved with a callback time moves to that time. It appears in Later today (or on its day) with a Callback label, ahead of the tool's own suggestions.

Without a callback, the tool suggests the next call itself: the same day at least two hours after the last dial and within the call hours, or else the next working day near the household's usual hour.

A8A complete under 3 minutes

Box 3.0 counts a complete only when the interview lasted at least 3 minutes. A shorter one counts as an appointment contact, so the household stays open and the tool tells you why.

A9Log a call made earlier

If you made a call without the timer, for example on another phone, use Log a call made earlier under the phone numbers. Enter which number, when the call started and how many minutes it lasted, then the result as in A6. The log keeps both the call's own time and the time you entered it.

A10Correct or void an entry

Nothing in the log is ever deleted. To fix a mistake, find the call under Calls this round and click Correct or void:

  • change the result, the number, the start time, the minutes, the callback time or the note; or
  • tick Void this entry if the call was logged by mistake.

Write why you are correcting it (required) and click Save correction. The original entry stays in the log, marked as corrected or voided. An action such as a release is undone the same way, with Undo.

A11Release, switch or removal

The Actions at the bottom of a household's panel are case actions, not call results:

  • Release for this round: stop calling the household this round. It stays in the panel and is called again next round (the handbook's rule is to release, never delete; Section 3.3.5). The reason is optional.
  • Ask for another enumerator: ask your leader to give the household to someone else next round, for example after a poor contact. Your leader decides.
  • Ask the leader to remove: only when no number works, or the household has no number at all. Your leader decides.

A12Add or correct a number

If a respondent gives a new number, or a number is wrong, click Add or correct a number. Choose which number (or a new one) and whether you correct it or mark it as not working, type the number and whose it is, and click Save number. The new number can be dialled at once. It goes to your leader in a separate contacts file when you export (A19), never in the log for the common folder.

A13Replacements

Some households come with replacement households, listed in their panel in the order the sample was drawn. A replacement can be used only when the round's rules allow it, by default after a hard refusal or when no number works. Then:

  1. Click Activate the next replacement.
  2. Write why (required) and confirm. The replacement becomes a household to call, and the original household is closed for the round.

When the button is greyed out, the line beside it says why, for example that replacements are allowed only after a hard refusal or when no number works.

A14The Households tab

The Households tab lists all your households in a table: their contact, why each is with you, the first dial planned, the number of dials, the last result, the next step and the state. Use the filters above it (open, not started, in progress, complete, released or closed, needs the leader, all) and the search box for an ID or a name. Click a row to open the household.

A15The Calendar tab

The Calendar shows the round's window month by month. Days inside the window are white; days outside are grey. Hatched days get no calls planned automatically: days the call center does not work, and public holidays. Each day shows the first dials planned, callbacks booked, and the dials made and completes. Click a day to see its calls.

Holidays are left out of the automatic plan but never block calling: you can still call and log on a holiday. Your leader can mark a holiday as a working day.

A16My performance

My performance gives your own figures, with the definitions of Box 3.0: dials, completes, success rate, dials per complete, first-dial success, enumerator-days and completes per enumerator-day. The columns compare this round so far with this month, last month and all the logs loaded. Click Load earlier logs and choose your exports from earlier rounds to fill the comparison.

When my calls succeed shows, by hour, the share of your dials on which the interview took place. Hours with fewer than 10 dials are hatched: too few to judge. The plan ahead counts the first dials, redials and callbacks for the coming days.

A17The Log tab

The Log lists every entry in your session: each call, action, correction and number change, who logged it and when. Its integrity check confirms that no entry was changed after it was logged. You cannot edit the log here; corrections are new entries (A10).

A18Save, close and continue

Save your session whenever you stop, and at least every few calls. Click Save session:

  • In Chrome, the first save asks where to put the file, named like E07_project_R05.json, in your own folder; later saves overwrite it.
  • In other browsers, each save downloads a new file with the time in its name.

Keep saved sessions in your own folder, not the common one. If your session file is protected, it is protected with your assignment's passphrase. After ten unsaved changes, a reminder appears.

Close session asks first when changes are not saved: Save, then close (the main choice), Close without saving, or Cancel. The browser also warns you before you close the page with unsaved changes.

To continue later, open the tool, click Continue a saved session on the start page (Figure A1) and choose your saved session file. Everything comes back, and the tool checks that the file was not changed.

A19Export your log

At the end of each day, and at the end of the round, click Export log:

  1. Download both files, named like E07_2026-10-27.csv and E07_2026-10-27.json.
  2. Put both in the common folder. They hold household IDs, times and results, but no names or phone numbers.
  3. If you added or corrected a number, a third file, E07_2026-10-27_contacts.json, is offered. Give it to your leader directly, never in the common folder.
Each export holds the whole round so far, so a newer export replaces an older one. Exporting does not replace saving: save your session as well.

A20An updated assignment

Your leader may send a new version of your assignment during the round, for example after moving households between enumerators so that the round finishes on time. Click Load updated assignment and choose the new file. The tool keeps every call you logged, adds the new households, and marks those no longer yours as “No longer yours”: their calls stay in your log, but you cannot log new ones for them. A file for another enumerator or another round is refused, and so is a file older than the one in your session.

A household moved to you shows who had it before and the calls they made, with the reason “Moved to you mid-round”. Those calls count toward the dial cap: if the previous enumerator made 7 of 8, you have one left. Its first dial is planned for the day of the move, at the household's usual hour. Households you already had keep their plan and history even when the new file does not repeat them.

A21On a phone

The page also works on a phone's browser. The columns stack, and a household's panel fills the screen; close it with the ✕ to return to the list.

A22Good practice and messages

  • Start the timer when you dial and end it when the call ends: the times are part of the data.
  • Save whenever you stop; export at the end of each day.
  • Never write personal details in notes. Keep your assignment and saved sessions in your own folder.
  • Use a release, not a refusal code, to stop calling a household for the round.

Part B · Team leaderPreparing a round, giving out files, importing exports, following progress

B1Start or continue a session

Click Team leader at the top of the page. To prepare a new round, fill in Start a round:

  1. your leader ID, for example L01, and the program code, for example L2MN;
  2. the country, which picks the holiday calendar;
  3. the round number. Click Start.

To carry on, click Continue a saved leader session and choose your session file (B11). If you only want to import logs for a round you prepared earlier, start the round the same way and open its manifest on the Import tab (B12).

B2The Prepare tab

The session opens on Prepare round, eight numbered steps from top to bottom. The other tabs are Import logs, Progress, Call log, Analytics and Risk lists. The number on Save leader session counts the changes not yet saved.

B3Step 1 · Household list

Click Open the household list and choose the program's list: a CSV file in UTF-8, or an Excel workbook (its first sheet). One row per household, with these columns:

ColumnContent
hhid, psu, stratumThe household's ID, its sampling unit and stratum
phone_1 … phone_3Up to three numbers; phone_k_label says whose each is
contact_name, respondent_member, language, tzOptional
statusactive (the default), removed (kept in the list, never deleted) or reserve (a replacement not yet used)
The household list holds contact information. Keep it in your own folder; it never goes in the common folder.

B4Step 2 · Earlier rounds

Click Add files and choose the files of earlier rounds, all at once:

  • the merged files this tool wrote for earlier rounds (_calls.csv, _actions.csv and _manifest.json); or
  • a program's own dial log in the same columns; or
  • interview records with hhid, round, interview_date, outcome, enumerator.

They give each household's last interview, last enumerator and dials, which the rule and the plan use. The table shows, for each round, the households, dials and interviewed households. If a program's own result codes are not the tool's, the tool asks you to map each one once.

B5Step 3 · Replacement list

Click Open the replacement list and choose either the sampling tool's selection.csv (rtmtools: unit_id, stratum, selected, reserve) or a program file with hhid, replacement_hhid, rank. Each PSU's reserve is dealt in turn to its households in selection order, so each replacement belongs to one household. Replacement households need their contacts in the household list.

Under the summary, tick Replacements in use this round and choose when they are allowed: after a hard refusal, when no number works, or when the dial cap is reached.

B6Step 4 · Round calendar

  1. Set the round's first day and last day (a fixed window) and the dial cap, the program's stopping rule in dials. The team, dial cap and call hours are taken from the last round's manifest when one is loaded.
  2. Tick the working days and set the call hours and the time zone.
  3. Choose over what share of the working days the first dials are spread (50% by default), to front-load the contact effort (Section 3.4).
  4. Set the day list: how many households each enumerator's Today tab offers at most each day. It is taken from the last round's manifest when one is loaded; empty, it becomes 1.5 × the busiest enumerator's planned first dials per day, shown once the rule is applied (step 6). The enumerator sees the calls due first, then households pulled forward from later days (“Ahead of plan”, A3), so that unanswered calls leave nobody waiting.
  5. Check the holidays inside the window. They come from a table bundled with the tool, built from the Python package holidays. Tick Call center works for a holiday you will work, remove a day that is not a holiday, or add a day off, such as a regional or decreed closure. Dates the table estimates, such as lunar holidays, are labelled; correct them when the official dates are known.

When the window falls in Ramadan, the card also asks for the hour the fast is broken; that hour is kept free of planned calls.

B7Step 5 · Team

The team comes from the last round's manifest, or is added with Add an enumerator ID. For each enumerator:

  • untick Available this round for someone who is away. They keep their history; their households are dealt to the others with the reason “enumerator unavailable”;
  • set a share: relative weights (1 each by default), or percentages adding up to 100 under the “share of total” rule;
  • remove someone who has left.

Matching criteria. Above the table, the card offers the columns of the household list that can decide who calls whom: language, and any other column with few distinct values, such as the respondent's sex, a dialect or an area (add the column to the household list; nothing else changes). Tick a criterion and a column appears in the team table: type each enumerator's values, several separated by spaces or commas (mn kk). A household then goes only to an enumerator whose values include the household's; a household without a value can go to anyone. Every available enumerator needs a value for every criterion ticked. The criteria and the values are written into the manifest and the assignment files, so the program can say how it assigned, and the next round offers them again.

B8Step 6 · Assignment rule

Under the default rule, enter the rotation share for this round, for example 10%. It has no default: the handbook calls its size “a judgment, not an estimate from these data”. A household is hard to reach when it was not interviewed last round although it received the full dial cap, or when its enumerator asked for a switch.

The seed is written into every file, so the same inputs and seed always give the same assignment. Click Apply the rule. If something is missing, such as the window or the rotation share, the tool lists it above the button.

If you change a setting after applying the rule (the team, the seed, the share, the window, the working days or the holidays), the tool sets the assignment and any files written from it aside and asks you to apply the rule again. Files and assignment always match the settings shown.

B9Step 7 · Check the assignment

Before any file is written, step 7 shows each enumerator's households: by reason (kept, rotation, hard to reach, enumerator away, new, or by the round's rule), by stratum, and in total. It also shows the expected dials, computed from each household's dials last round, against the enumerator's target share.

  • Loads outside the margin (15% by default) are marked. Move kept households at random to balance moves kept households from the busiest to the least busy enumerators, with the reason “capacity”, until every load is within the margin.
  • Open Households: move one by hand to change any household's enumerator; the move is recorded as “by hand”.
  • With matching criteria (B7), a kept household whose enumerator does not match moves with the reason “matched”, the balance never moves a household to an enumerator who does not match it, and the card says whether every household is with a matching enumerator. A household that no available enumerator matches is listed at the top of the household table without an enumerator: choose one by hand (it is marked as not matched), or give an enumerator the value in step 5. No file is written while a household has no enumerator.

Households without a last interview are planned at the program's best hours from earlier rounds (Section 3.5.1). New households, households switched as hard to reach and households in the high dropout tier (B18) get the earliest first dials.

B10Step 8 · Write and give out the files

  1. Tick Protect each assignment file with its own passphrase if a file could end up in the wrong place. The tool makes one passphrase per enumerator and shows them once: copy them or click Download the passphrase list. They are not saved anywhere.
  2. Click Write the files. If some loads are still outside the margin, a reminder names them; you can still write.
  3. Click Save all into a folder (Chrome) or Download all.

Each assignment file holds only its enumerator's households and their replacements, with their contacts. No file carries another enumerator's contact information.

B11Save and close the session

Click Save leader session to keep the inputs, settings, imports and every decision in one file, leader_L2MN-DEMO_R05.json. To protect it, type a passphrase twice; leave both empty to save it unprotected. The file holds the household list with its contacts: keep it in your own folder.

B12Import the exports

Whenever you want to see progress, open the Import logs tab.

  1. If the session has no manifest yet (for example a new session for importing only), click Open a round manifest and choose the manifest written in step 8.
  2. Click Add files and choose the enumerators' exports from the common folder, all at once: their .json and .csv files, and any contacts files they gave you. Adding older exports does no harm: the newest export of each enumerator is used.

B13Merge questions and the merge report

The merge never decides silently. Each situation it finds is a line in the merge report; those that need you appear under Needs your decision, with a button for each choice and an optional note. A decision is logged with who took it and when, and can be reviewed in the report.

The Files table shows how each file was used. Download the merge report keeps the record of every situation, the rule applied and your decisions.

B14New or corrected numbers

Numbers that enumerators added or corrected arrive in their contacts files. Download contact updates gives them in one table; move them into the household list for the next round. The file holds phone numbers: keep it in your own folder.

B15Progress

The Progress tab follows the round:

  • Team: each enumerator's households by state, dials, enumerator-days, share complete and completes per enumerator-day (Box 3.0), with the time of their last export.
  • Completes over the window: cumulative completes by day, and completion by stratum.
  • Households: every household with its enumerator, reason, state, dials and last result, filtered by state or enumerator.

Move waiting households

Part-way through the round, the card Move waiting households shows, for each enumerator, the completes per enumerator-day so far (the pace), how many they can still complete in the working days left, and whether they are short or have spare days at that pace. Only enumerators whose latest export is from today, with two or more enumerator-days, are judged; tick the box to judge earlier exports too.

  1. Choose which households may move: those not yet called (the default), or also those called without a booked callback or a hard refusal. A complete, a released or replaced household and a replacement never move.
  2. Click Propose moves. Households move one at a time from the enumerator furthest behind to the one with most spare days, until nobody is more than half a household off; the latest-planned households go first, and only to an enumerator who matches their criteria (B7). Untick a move to leave that household where it is, or add one by hand by its ID.
  3. Click Write the updated files. The tool writes a new assignment file for each enumerator who gives or receives a household (revision 2, 3…) and the next revision of the manifest, which lists every move with when and by whom. Give each file to its enumerator, who opens it with Load updated assignment (A20). This needs the household list open in the session (step 1).

No household is in two files, and no double call goes unnoticed: a moved household carries the dials its previous enumerator made, which count toward the dial cap, and in the merge the previous enumerator's dials up to the move are theirs while any made after it is flagged for you to keep or leave out.

B16Call log and the round's files

B18Risk lists

The Risk lists tab is for the leader only. Two models rank households to order the work:

  • Dropout risk ranks last round's respondents by how likely they are to be missed this round. It was trained on the handbook's six programs with dial logs.
  • Return likelihood ranks the households interviewed two rounds ago and missed last round by how likely they are to come back. It was trained on two programs and is weak: use it only to order redials.

Tiers are tied to workload: within the round, the top 15% by score are high, the next 35% medium and the rest low (the return model has only high and low). A tier says how similar households fared; it is never a prediction about one household and never a reason to drop one (Section 3.3.5). Enumerators never see tiers, and tiers are not written into assignment files; the high dropout tier only gets earlier first dials in the plan. The models use operational features only, never demographic ones.

Each list shows the household, its enumerator, its tier, its score against the round's average, and what moves the score most. Use the tier buttons to filter, and Download this list to keep it.

This program's own data. Applied to a new program, the models rank households usefully but their chances are not calibrated, so the list shows a score, not a chance. Once two rounds have outcomes, click Backtest on this program's own rounds. The tool reads the result out in words, from the backtest's 95% interval:

  • “too few outcomes to judge”: keep the version in use and backtest again after the next round;
  • “worse than chance”: do not use the lists for this program;
  • otherwise it says whether the recalibrated version brings the chances closer to what happened.

Choose use on a version only if the backtest supports it; the switch is logged. The model card at the bottom of the tab gives the training data, the features, the backtests and the models' limits.

Part C · With KoBoToolbox, a call-center system, Survey Solutions and a calling serviceWhen enumerators record their calls in a data-collection platform or a call-center system rather than in this tool

C1When this part applies

Every L2 program with phone rounds interviews in KoBoToolbox, and they record their calls in two ways. The Philippines program submits its interview form once per call attempt, whether or not anyone answered. The Central Asian programs record no attempts in the form: a call-center system holds the sample, shows each operator the households to call, dials, and logs every attempt with a status code; the form records the interview only. In both cases the leader's side of this tool works the same: you prepare the round, give the platform its list, import each day's export, follow progress and move households during the round. The tool reads the platform's export as it is and writes the list in the platform's layout. Nothing is typed twice.

What a program needs: its form as it is, the household list in the tool's columns (B3), the team (B7) with each enumerator's code in the platform, and, for a form the tool does not know, the mapping of its columns and codes once (C4). Each round: write the files (B10), give the platform its list (C5), and each day drop the platform's export on the Import tab.

Platform codes. Where the platform knows an enumerator by another code than their ID here (an interviewer number, an operator code such as CATI07), enter it on the Prepare tab, step 5, Platform code. The tool then reads the platform's records under the enumerator's ID and writes the code into the lists it gives the platform.

C2A form that records each call attempt

The Philippines program's form asks, before the interview, the interviewer's ID, the household (picked from the sample list in the form's choices sheet), the attempt number and whether someone answered; after the interview or instead of it, the result of the call in ten codes (complete; partially complete with or without a callback; not started, callback scheduled; refused; nobody answering; number does not exist; phone off; household unknown; reference person cannot connect) and remarks. KoBoToolbox adds the time the form was opened and submitted.

Download the data (Data › Downloads › CSV with XML values and headers, or the project's synchronous export link) and add it under Add platform files on the Import tab. The tool recognises the columns, reads each submission as one dial with the result mapped onto the codes of Part A (R1: the program's "partially complete, no more callback" counts as a complete, as the program counts it), splits the file by interviewer and merges each part as it merges a CSV export (B13): unverified, cumulative, the latest file used, every merge question raised where it applies. A complete needs a form open for three minutes or more.

What goes back: on the Call log tab, choices_households.csv is the sample list in the layout of the form's choices sheet, one row per household and unused replacement, with the interviewer's platform code in filter_int_id. Paste it over the form's list and redeploy; with a choice_filter of filter_int_id = ${int_id} on the household question, each interviewer sees only their households. The list has no phone numbers.

C3A call-center system's log and the interview form

The Tajikistan program's system exports one semicolon-separated table, cumulative across rounds: operator, household, round, number dialed, start, end, a comment and a status code (SCS successful, NOA no answer, NOT no time, NWR non-working number, WNM wrong number, OOS out of service, ITR interrupted, RFS refused; two codes without a label in the files seen are read as "other" until the program names them). Add the day's download under Add platform files: only the round's rows are read, the operators' codes become the enumerators' IDs, the times are placed in the round's time zone, and the number dialed becomes the household's slot (number 1, 2 or 3) through the household list, which must be open; the number itself is not kept. The Uzbekistan program's system exports a similar table with its own codes, which the tool reads too.

The interview form of such a program records no attempts, so its export is not counted as calls. Added with the log, it is checked against it: each interview with consent is matched to a completed call of its household within three hours of its start; an interview without one, and a completed call without an interview, are listed under Needs your decision (count it, keep it, or recode it as "other"), and the Import tab sums up the matching. Without a log, the form's export is read as the completes and refusals.

What goes back: cati_assignments.csv, the assignment table for the system, one row per household and unused replacement with the operator's code, the planned first dial, the numbers and the replacement links. It holds phone numbers and goes to the system only.

C4A form the tool does not know

A file whose columns match none of the layouts above is not merged; the Import tab shows a card for it instead (Figure C4). Say whether a row is a call attempt or an interview, which column plays each role (household, enumerator, start and result are needed; end, attempt, callback, number dialed, round, record ID, submission time and note are optional), and what each value of the result column means, chosen from the results of R1; the card lists the values with their counts. Save the mapping and merge the file keeps the mapping in your session, so that every later file with the same columns is read without asking.

C5A round, day by day

  1. Before the round. Prepare it as in B3 to B10, with each enumerator's platform code (step 5). On the Call log tab, write choices_households.csv (a form with its list in the choices sheet), cati_assignments.csv (a call-center system), households.csv (the tool's own form, C6) or assignments.tab (Survey Solutions), and give it to the platform.
  2. Each day. Download the platform's export and add it under Add platform files. Progress (B15), the call log (B16) and the analytics (B17) follow, and every merge question of B13 is raised where it applies: a call on another enumerator's household, two completes, a call after a move, an enumerator without records, an interview without a logged call.
  3. Part-way through. Move waiting households (B15, Move waiting households). Then write the list again from the new manifest and give it to the platform: the moved households appear with their new enumerator, and a call the previous enumerator makes after the move is flagged as a double call.
  4. Next round. The merged call log and the actions file of this round are the history the next round's assignment reads (B4), whichever platform recorded them.

C6Survey Solutions and a calling service

No L2 program used either; both are read as their documentation describes. A team without a form of its own can use the tool's call-record form, formats/kobo/rtm_call_record.xlsx (one submission per call, the household picked from the media file households.csv that the Call log tab writes), or the same questionnaire built in Survey Solutions Designer from formats/suso/README.md, whose assignments upload assignments.tab the tool writes and whose export zip it opens in the browser. Where calls go through an internet calling service, its call log is a second witness: the example is Twilio Programmable Voice, whose Call resource gives for every call the number called, the start and end, the duration and the status; the tool matches each service call to a recorded call by the household's number and a start within three minutes, fills a call end the enumerator left blank, lists the calls to a panel number that nobody recorded, and counts the calls to numbers outside the list. The numbers never leave your computer.
